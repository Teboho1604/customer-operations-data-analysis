Customer Operations Analysis — Findings Report

1. Overview

The analysis examined customer-service records to identify patterns in customer satisfaction, resolution times, repeat contacts, products, issue types, and service channels.

The objective was to identify operational areas that may require further investigation and improvement.

2. Key Performance Areas

The analysis focused on the following measures:

- Total customer-service cases
- Average resolution time
- Average customer satisfaction
- Repeat-contact rate
- Issue-type frequency
- Channel performance
- Product-level performance

3. Data Quality Observations

Initial data-quality checks were performed before interpreting the results.

The checks included:

- Duplicate customer identifiers
- Missing satisfaction scores
- Missing resolution times
- Inconsistent issue-type categories
- Customer-service channel values
- Satisfaction-score ranges
- Long resolution times

One example of a categorical inconsistency identified in the dataset was the use of both "Technical" and "technical".

This type of inconsistency can affect grouping and reporting if it is not standardized before analysis.

4. Customer Satisfaction

Customer satisfaction was analyzed across issue types, products, and customer-service channels.

Lower satisfaction scores were examined alongside resolution times and repeat contacts to identify cases that may require further investigation.

5. Resolution Time

Resolution time was analyzed to determine whether longer customer-service interactions were associated with lower satisfaction.

Cases exceeding 60 minutes were specifically reviewed because they represent longer-than-average interactions within this sample.

6. Repeat Customer Contacts

Repeat contacts were analyzed by product to identify areas where customers may require additional assistance after their initial interaction.

A high repeat-contact rate can be useful as an operational indicator because it may highlight issues that are not being fully resolved during the first interaction.

7. Potential At-Risk Cases

Potentially at-risk cases were identified using two indicators:

- Customer satisfaction score of 2 or below
- A repeat customer contact

These indicators do not prove that a customer will leave the company. Instead, they identify records that could be prioritized for further investigation.

8. Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis framework, the company could consider:

1. Investigating customer cases with consistently low satisfaction scores.
2. Reviewing cases with unusually long resolution times.
3. Investigating products with higher repeat-contact rates.
4. Standardizing categorical data before reporting.
5. Monitoring customer-service channels for differences in satisfaction and resolution performance.
6. Developing regular data-quality checks for operational reporting.

9. Limitations

This project uses a fictional dataset created for portfolio and learning purposes.

The results therefore should not be interpreted as representing the performance of a real company.

The analysis identifies patterns within the sample but does not establish causation. Additional data would be required to determine why customers are dissatisfied or why repeat contacts occur.

10. Next Steps

Future analysis could include:

- Building an Excel dashboard
- Adding monthly performance trends
- Segmenting customers by additional characteristics
- Creating automated data-quality checks
- Comparing first-contact resolution across channels
- Developing a recurring operational reporting process
